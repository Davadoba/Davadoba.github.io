Risk Resource

Explainable natural-disaster risk scores for every U.S. state and county. I built the backend.

  • FastAPI service scoring 3,221 counties from live NWS, USGS, NOAA and Open-Meteo feeds plus FEMA baselines
  • Stays up when government APIs don't: per-key caching, stale-data fallback, retry backoff
  • Lambda functions on an EventBridge schedule keep the weather data fresh
How it works

Each hazard score is half long-term baseline and half live signal, kept apart so every score can be explained in plain language. The overall score weights the worst hazard most heavily, so a place with high flood risk doesn't look safe just because it never sees earthquakes. When a source fails, the API serves data up to an hour old and says it's stale, instead of quietly reporting "no alerts".

Python, FastAPI, Supabase, AWS Lambda, EventBridge

Granny Green heart-rate study

Making wearable heart-rate data trustworthy, and making the study platform faster and more natural for participants.

  • Quality-control protocol that validates each window of PPG data against respiration, movement and published thresholds
  • Whisper speech recognition down to about 0.6s per response at roughly 93% accuracy in manual testing
  • Parser so participants can say "forty-two" instead of "four, two"
How it works

Signals from an arm-worn sensor are easily corrupted by movement and breathing, and if bad windows slip through, every analysis after them inherits the error. The protocol flags bursts of movement, cross-checks breathing, and documents where it holds and where it doesn't, so the next research assistant can adapt it instead of starting over. On the platform side I also fixed words being read as the wrong language and wrote unit tests for the transcription module.

Python, Flask, Whisper, EmotiBit

Project OVERLAP

Pairing news about tariffs, AI and healthcare with the economics research that puts it in context.

  • Evidence base built from every Journal of Economic Perspectives paper since 2000
  • LLM pipeline with Claude that turns each paper into claim cards: quote, claim, conditions, numbers, citation
  • Currently Investigating Independently: graph-based representations of claims, evidence, reasoning, etc. to create a more grounded and reliable representation of an academic corpus that AI agents can use.
Why claim cards

When research does reach the news, the caveats usually get lost. Keeping each claim next to the conditions it holds under means a reader can see not just what a paper found, but when that finding applies.

Python, Claude API

NBA trade prediction

Can XGBoost tell who gets traded this offseason? I'm leading a group project to find out which player and team patterns predict a trade.

  • One row per player, team and season for every player under contract from 2015–16 to 2025–26, merged from Basketball-Reference, ESPN, the NBA API and a Hugging Face dataset
  • Features cover the player (regular-season and playoff stats, 3-year trends, age), the team (seed, playoff runs, 5-year history) and the salary cap (cap share, years left, tax and apron status)
  • In progress: testing XGBoost against an 8% base-rate baseline on PR-AUC, with SHAP to show which features drive a trade
Why these choices

Players on expiring contracts are left out, because they become free agents and are rarely traded in the offseason. Only about 8% of the remaining players get traded, so accuracy would reward a model that never predicts a trade. PR-AUC measures how well the model finds the players who actually do get moved, and the baseline gives every player the same 8% chance.

Python, XGBoost, pandas, SHAP
